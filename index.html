About Me

Connecting people through creativity

I have over 30 years in the creative sector and 12 years at Mind the Gap — one of the UK's leading learning disability theatre companies. My journey has been shaped by incredible people, bold ideas, and the power of lived experience.

Growing up in Dorset, I trained at Jellico Theatre where I gained my First Diploma in Performing Arts, and performed with Double Act Theatre Company — taking our work all around the UK.

At Mind the Gap, my biggest achievement was being Intern Assistant Producer for ZARA — Mind the Gap's biggest ever show about learning disabilities and parenthood. Working with the company gave me the confidence and skills to become who I am today.

Through my Routes Out research project, I investigated the barriers organisations face when hiring learning disabled and neurodivergent people — and worked to create solutions that actually work.

Today, I work across multiple areas — from facilitated workshops to access consultancy, event hosting to collaborative creative projects. Everything I do is rooted in my lived experience as a learning disabled person.

Featured Workshop: Confidence in Your Voice

A 60-minute workshop I deliver for learning disabled, disabled and neurodiverse people. Built around movement, games and conversation — helping people find and express their voice in a fun, supported environment.

💡 I always ask for a member of your team to be in the room to support who is in the room. No experience needed. Just bring yourself.

What to Expect

This workshop is about having fun, building confidence, and discovering your creative voice. You'll work with others in a space designed to be accessible, inclusive and joyful.

Workshop Schedule:

  1. Warm up (5 mins): Physical warm-up to get energy going and settle into the space
  2. Name Game (10 mins): Get to know the group in a fun, interactive way
  3. Pass the Sound (10 mins): Voice warm-up that's playful and confidence-building
  4. Zip Zap Boing (10 mins): Energy builder that gets everyone moving and laughing
  5. Hot Seating (15 mins): "Who Am I?" game exploring characters and perspectives
  6. Choice Activities (8 mins): Participants pick activities and share in pairs
  7. Warm Down (2 mins): Close with calm, reflection and appreciation

In Development

Tap Out Never Submit

Co-Writer & Performer

Tap Out Never Submit is a brand new radio play. It tells the story of two learning disabled brothers who love wrestling. One brother is living in a care home under strict restrictions and coercive control, while the other fights to help him.

This story is inspired by my feelings as a learning disabled person watching the Panorama documentary about Winterbourne View — not from personal experience, but from the fear it stirred about what could happen to me.

The play also takes in the stories from 2026 of families being torn apart — because this is not just history. It is still happening now. Through wrestling, brotherhood and determination, this play explores care, control and what freedom really means.
