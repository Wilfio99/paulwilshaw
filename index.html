About Me

Connecting people through creativity

I have over 30 years in the creative sector and 12 years at Mind the Gap — one of the UK's leading learning disability theatre companies. I moved from Wimborne in Dorset to Bradford, and that journey has shaped everything I do.

Growing up in Dorset, I trained at Jellico Theatre where I gained my First Diploma in Performing Arts, and performed with Double Act Theatre Company — taking on roles including the Tin Man in The Wizard of Oz and the March Hare in Alice in Wonderland. In 2012 I performed in the Paralympic Closing Ceremony in London and was Assistant Producer for Breathe, the opening ceremony for the sailing events in Weymouth at the 2012 Olympics.

At Mind the Gap, my biggest achievement was being Intern Assistant Producer for ZARA — Mind the Gap's biggest ever show about learning disabilities and parenthood. I progressed from student artist through to engagement and advocacy work, developing a unique perspective on what it means to make the arts truly accessible.

Through my Routes Out research project, I investigated the barriers organisations face when hiring learning disabled and neurodivergent people — and worked to change them. Now working independently as a consultant, facilitator, MC, panellist and DJ, I connect the dots between artists, organisations and communities — making sure no one gets left behind.

Featured Workshop

Confidence in Your Voice

A 60-minute workshop I deliver for learning disabled, disabled and neurodiverse people. Built around movement, games and conversation — helping people find and use their voice in a safe, fun environment.

I always have a support person in the room. No experience needed. Just bring yourself.

Workshop Schedule:

  1. Warm up: Physical warm-up to get energy going
  2. Name Game: Get to know the group
  3. Pass the Sound: Voice warm-up
  4. Zip Zap Boing: Energy builder
  5. Hot Seating: Who Am I? game
  6. Choice Activities: Pick and explain in pairs
  7. Warm Down: Close with calm

In Development

Tap Out Never Submit

Co-Writer & Performer

Tap Out Never Submit is a brand new radio play. It tells the story of two learning disabled brothers who love wrestling. One brother is living in a care home miles away from his family, where bad things are happening. Wrestling is paramount to the story — it becomes the language of the play, used in place of depicting the abuse directly. Will the brother speak out? What will happen to the family and those closest to them?

This story is inspired by my feelings as a learning disabled person watching the Panorama documentary about Winterbourne View — not from personal experience of the care system, but from the anger and sadness I felt watching it and the need to give voice to something that affects our community.

The play also takes in the stories from 2026 of families being torn apart — because this is not just history. It is still happening now.
